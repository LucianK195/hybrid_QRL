# Conditional quantum-assisted advantage study

## Purpose

This study asks two separate questions:

1. Can the complete hybrid pipeline execute safely with candidates generated by
   dense, QuTiP, and the downloaded geometry-driven neutral-atom backend?
2. Is there a hardware-compatible regime in which a blockade sampler offers an
   acceptable quality-versus-diversity-or-latency tradeoff against tuned
   classical search?

The first question is an engineering pipeline proof. The second is a
conditional empirical advantage test. Neither is a claim of asymptotic quantum
supremacy.

## Claim ladder

Results must be interpreted in the following order:

1. **Safety proof:** backend measurements can pass through repair, learned-Q
   reranking, and environment execution without violating the application
   graph.
2. **Surrogate opportunity:** a scalable classical blockade surrogate finds a
   regime with at least 90% reference return, competitive epsilon-5% coverage,
   and a latency or diversity improvement.
3. **Calibration transfer:** the surrogate distribution remains sufficiently
   close to small-system quantum evolution.
4. **Geometry-backend quality:** the downloaded backend maintains at least 90%
   critic-selected reference return.

Only a pass at every level would justify moving to a hardware conditional-
advantage claim. A surrogate success cannot bypass calibration.

## Sampler-in-the-loop learning

The direct actor-critic samples actions from its graph-masked Bernoulli policy.
The new training path instead executes:

```text
state
  -> learned actor utilities
  -> Rydberg-blockade proposal batch
  -> application-graph repair
  -> learned Q-critic best-of-K selection
  -> dispatch transition and reward
```

The discrete sampler has no analytic gradient in this implementation. Actor
weights are therefore trained with paired simultaneous perturbation stochastic
approximation. At iteration `k`, a random direction `d_k` generates two matched
rollouts:

```text
J_plus  = J(theta + c_k d_k)
J_minus = J(theta - c_k d_k)
g_k     = (J_plus - J_minus) d_k / (2 c_k)
```

The rollouts share environment and sampler seeds to reduce variance. The state
and action critics are fitted to Monte Carlo returns from both trajectories.
MILP, beam, greedy, and teacher actions never enter the updates.

## Epsilon-optimal metrics

For each safe action, realized immediate dispatch reward is divided by the
zero-gap MILP reference. For epsilon in 1%, 5%, and 10%, the study reports:

- `p_epsilon`: probability or empirical candidate fraction with reward ratio at
  least `1 - epsilon`;
- `coverage_k`: probability of at least one epsilon-optimal sample in K iid
  shots; and
- `K95`: shots required for 95% success,

```text
K95 = ceil(log(0.05) / log(1 - p_epsilon)).
```

`K95` is undefined when `p_epsilon = 0`. It assumes independent shots; hardware
autocorrelation requires an effective-sample-size correction.

## Backend calibration

The calibration uses 20 held-out states at each size:

| Size | Backends |
|---|---|
| 8 | dense, QuTiP, downloaded manual backend, surrogate |
| 10 | dense, QuTiP, downloaded manual backend, surrogate |
| 12 | QuTiP, downloaded manual backend, surrogate |

Dense 12-qubit evolution is omitted because repeated dense 4096-by-4096 matrix
exponentials are not a responsible benchmark. QuTiP becomes the n=12 reference.

Dense basis index bit `i` maps to graph node `i`. QuTiP and the downloaded
backend use node zero as the most-significant tensor digit. The calibration
normalizes these conventions before computing total-variation distance.

Every physical register is scaled to unit minimum atom separation. This avoids
numerically extreme interactions from nearly coincident random points. The
manual backend still computes every `C6/r^6` pair interaction, while dense and
QuTiP receive exact graph edges. Their difference is deliberately retained and
reported as physical-model mismatch.

## Phase-map design

The 16-decision search uses 20 selection seeds and 20 independent confirmation
seeds per regime. It covers 39 structured regimes:

- 27 physical cells from graph density 0.06/0.12/0.22, physical blockade-radius
  scale 0.9/1.0/1.1, and short/balanced/adiabatic schedules;
- nine graph-structure cells from unit-disk/grid/clustered geometry crossed with
  none/spatial/degree utility correlation; and
- uniform, lognormal, and bimodal utility distributions.

Each cell compares K=16 beam search, local search, simulated annealing, and the
Rydberg surrogate. The surrogate opportunity gate requires:

- mean critic-selected reward/reference ratio at least 0.90;
- epsilon-5% batch-hit rate no worse than beam and local search; and
- either lower latency or at least 5% greater Hamming diversity than both.

Before any surrogate trend can support a quantum claim, mean calibration TV
must be at most 0.15 and the geometry-driven manual backend must reach at least
0.90 mean critic-selected ratio.

## Current result

The experiment produced:

- 40 paired training-comparison episodes;
- 220 backend calibration records;
- 40 dynamic pipeline-proof episodes; and
- 6,240 phase-map method records: 3,120 selection and 3,120 confirmation.

Sampler-in-loop training improved matched held-out episode return by
`0.433 ± 0.070`. Every executed action from all four backend paths was safe.

The selection split chose density 0.06, physical radius scale 1.0, balanced
pulse schedule at `0.969 ± 0.014` critic-selected reference return. On the
independent confirmation split, it retained `0.958` mean return and greater
diversity than beam search, but its epsilon-5% batch-hit rate was 0.90 versus
beam's 0.95. Its latency was also higher than beam search. The strict surrogate
opportunity gate therefore did not pass confirmation.

The transfer gate failed. Mean surrogate TV distance to the dense/QuTiP
reference was 0.482, and the downloaded manual backend achieved only 0.789 mean
critic-selected reference ratio. The scientifically valid conclusion is:

> The complete simulated hybrid pipeline works safely, and the surrogate shows
> an exploratory diversity-quality tradeoff, but neither the strict confirmed
> opportunity gate nor calibrated quantum-assisted advantage is established.

## Reproduction

From the workspace root:

```powershell
$env:PYTHONPATH = ".\hybrid_qrl\src"
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe -B `
  .\hybrid_qrl\experiments\dispatch.py conditional `
  --seeds 20 `
  --training-iterations 140 `
  --calibration-seeds 20 `
  --phase-seeds 40 `
  --k 16
```

Outputs:

- `results/conditional_advantage_results.json`: configuration, learned weights,
  histories, per-instance distributions and metrics, phase records, and gate
  decisions;
- `results/conditional_advantage_report.md`: aggregate results with 95%
  confidence intervals and the claim-boundary decision.

## Next experiment

The held-out gate must remain unchanged. Optimize only on new training and
validation seeds:

1. embed application graphs using minimum-separation-aware atom placement;
2. learn the utility-to-local-detuning scale and offset;
3. optimize manual-backend global/local pulse amplitudes and duration against
   environment return;
4. add geometry mismatch and readout noise during sampler-in-loop training; and
5. rerun the same calibration transfer, manual-quality, and classical-baseline
   gates once.

If those gates fail again, the appropriate claim remains safe hybrid-pipeline
feasibility rather than conditional quantum-assisted advantage.
